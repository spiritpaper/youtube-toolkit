# Lab notebook

The lab notebook keeps what you notice, learn and test, and brings it back while you write. Open it from **Lab notebook** in the sidebar. It has three tabs: **Notes**, **Lessons** and **Experiments**.

## Notes

These are the timestamped notes you take on videos, from **Add note** in the [panel on YouTube](finding-outliers.md#the-video-panel) or in the [Script lab](scripts-and-titles.md#my-notes), all in one place and grouped by video. Click a note's time to open the video at that moment.

Add **#tags** anywhere in a note to group it, for example "Opens on the finished level #hook". Click a tag above the list to show only those notes, or type in **Filter**.

## Lessons

There are two kinds of lessons:
- **Your lessons:** write down what you learned, with #tags if you like. Ctrl+Enter saves.
- **From forecast vs reality:** after you publish a video you test-screened and run **Explain the differences** on its [Forecast vs reality](audience-lab.md#forecast-vs-reality-getting-better-with-every-video) card, the "For the next screening" lessons show up here automatically. **Keep & edit** turns one into your own lesson, so you can reword and tag it.

## Experiments

An experiment tests one idea across several videos, using the real numbers:

1. **New experiment.** Write the hypothesis ("Showing the finished game first gets more views"), what you'll do differently, and how to measure it:

   | Measure | Where it comes from |
   | --- | --- |
   | **Views at 7 days** (× channel median) | The project's [after-publishing tracking](idea-board.md). This is the fairest comparison, because every video is measured at the same age. |
   | **Views now** (× channel median) | The latest tracked snapshot |
   | **Average % watched**, **Click-through rate** | Videos checked in Forecast vs reality while signed in |

2. **Link the videos that try it**, planned or published. Results fill in as they're published and tracked.
3. **Read the results.** The experiment compares the median of its videos with the median of your other published videos, for example "These videos did 40% better than your other 12 published videos." With fewer than 3 videos, it says to treat the result as a hint, not proof.
4. **Conclude.** Set the status (confirmed, not confirmed or unclear) and write what you concluded.

## While you write

Under a project's **Outline** and **Script** there's a **From your notebook** box. As you write, it shows up to 4 notes, lessons and experiments that match the paragraph you're on and the heading above it. For example, under "## Hook" you'll see your #hook notes and the lessons about openings. Each item links back to its source (the video at that moment, the project, or the experiment) and lists the words that matched.

It's plain word matching on your device: no AI call and no quota. Rare words count more than common ones, and a matching #tag counts double. **Hide** collapses the box on every project, and **Show** brings it back.

Your notes, lessons and experiments are included in [backups](data-and-storage.md#backup-and-restore), and quick search (Ctrl+K) finds them too.
